F-number vs. lens curvature

The F-number n only sets the iris opening, d = f/n; the glass does not change. Curvature belongs to the focal length: for a single lens, 1/f = (ng − 1)(1/R1 − 1/R2), so a shorter f means more strongly curved surfaces, which is how the lens is drawn here. Camera zooms change f by moving groups of elements instead, and focusing moves the lens (changes b′). The human eye is the exception: it really does change its curvature to focus.

Thin lines are the three construction rays (center; parallel → F′; via F → parallel). The shaded cone is the light from the apple's top that passes the iris, drawn about 2× wider than scale so it stays visible. The numbers are exact.
